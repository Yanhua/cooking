**Serves:** 2

**Active time:** 25 minutes
**Total time:** About 1 hour 45 minutes

## Ingredients

- 250 g skin-on pork belly, chilled and hand-cut into 5 mm strips
- 12 g dried shiitake mushrooms (about 4 medium)
- 1 clove garlic, finely chopped
- 2 eggs
- 20 g fried Asian shallots (about 1/4 cup)
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 2 tsp (0.67 tbsp) dark soy sauce
- 4 tsp Shaoxing cooking wine
- 8 g Chinese rock sugar, lightly crushed
- 1 each star anise
- 1/2 each cinnamon stick
- 350 ml boiling water
- 1 bunch baby bok choy
- 150 g dry rice

## Source

- Primary: Maggie Zhu, “Lu Rou Fan (Taiwanese Pork Rice Bowl),” *Omnivore's Cookbook*, updated 25 October 2022, https://omnivorescookbook.com/lu-rou-fan/ (accessed 12 September 2026).
- Cross-check: Mandy Lee, “Taiwanese pork ragu on rice (lu rou fan),” adapted from *The Art of Escapism Cooking* and published by SBS Food, 21 June 2022, https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/taiwanese-pork-ragu-on-rice-lu-rou-fan/ps7m6gife (accessed 12 September 2026).

## Adaptations

- Scaled Zhu's four-serving recipe to two, retaining hand-cut skin-on pork belly, fried shallots, dried shiitake, soy, Shaoxing wine, rock sugar, star anise, cinnamon, and braised eggs.
- Uses ready-fried Asian shallots, an option given by Zhu, to keep active work within 25 minutes. Homemade fried shallots would be fresher but add substantial active time.
- Serves blanched bok choy rather than raw or pickled accompaniments to suit the household preference for cooked vegetables.
- Uses a small saucepan so the reduced quantity stays mostly submerged. Braising remains on the stovetop rather than under pressure because the slow reduction develops the characteristic glossy sauce.

## Method

1. Put the dried shiitake in a bowl with 250 ml of the boiling water; cover and soak for 20 minutes. Lift them out, squeeze gently, remove the tough stems, and slice the caps into 5 mm strips. Strain and reserve the soaking liquid, leaving any grit behind. Meanwhile, toss the chilled pork belly with 1 tsp of the Shaoxing wine and 1/2 tsp of the dark soy sauce.
2. Start the rice in the rice cooker according to its instructions. Boil the eggs for 9 minutes, cool under cold running water, and peel. Halve the bok choy lengthways; blanch it in the egg water for 1–2 minutes until tender, then drain.
3. Put the pork and its marinade in a dry, small heavy-based saucepan over medium heat. Cook for 5–7 minutes, stirring, until some fat renders and the edges are lightly browned. Stir in the garlic for 20 seconds.
4. Add the remaining 3 tsp Shaoxing wine, remaining 1 1/2 tsp dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, rock sugar, star anise, cinnamon, fried shallots, and shiitake. Stir for 30 seconds, then add the reserved mushroom liquid. Add up to the remaining 100 ml boiling water as needed so the pork is just covered.
5. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat to a very gentle simmer and partially cover. Braise for 60–75 minutes, stirring every 20 minutes. Add the peeled eggs for the final 30 minutes, turning once. The pork is ready when the skin and fat are yielding and the meat is tender; add a splash of hot water if the pan threatens to dry out before then.
6. Remove the eggs and whole spices. If the sauce is watery, simmer uncovered for 5–10 minutes until glossy and loose enough to soak into rice; if it becomes sticky, loosen it with hot water. Taste before adding any salt. Halve the eggs and spoon the pork, sauce, and egg over the rice with the blanched bok choy.
